Privacy Policy

Last updated October 17, 2026

Family Calendar is a Roku channel that shows your calendars, local weather, local news and photos of your town on your TV. This policy explains what information the channel uses and where it goes.

The short version

  • There are no accounts, sign-ins, ads or analytics.
  • Your settings are saved only on your Roku device. The developer never receives them.
  • To show weather, news and photos, the channel asks a few public services for information about the place you chose.

What is stored on your Roku

During setup you choose a location (a city, ZIP code or postal code), paste calendar links, and pick news topics and a colour theme. These are saved in your Roku device's own storage, along with a short list of town photos and copies of those photos. Nothing is sent to the developer. You can change or remove these settings any time from the channel's setup screen (press * on the remote). Removing the channel from your Roku deletes them.

Services the channel contacts

The channel connects directly from your Roku to the services below. Each one receives your device's IP address as part of any internet request, plus only the details listed.

ServiceUsed forWhat it receives
Open-MeteoWeather, and finding your town during setupYour location's coordinates; the place name or ZIP code you type
Zippopotam.usFinding your town from a Canadian postal codeThe first three characters of the postal code
Google NewsLocal and topic headlinesYour town, province or state, country and chosen topics
Wikipedia and Wikimedia CommonsPhotos of places near you, about once a weekYour location's coordinates
Your calendar provider (for example Google, Apple or Microsoft)Your eventsA request for the calendar link you added
Google CalendarPublic holidays for your countryA request for that country's public holiday calendar

Each service handles what it receives under its own privacy policy. Calendar links usually contain a private key. Anyone with the link can read that calendar, so the channel keeps it only on your device and shows only a shortened name on screen.

Roku

Roku, Inc. collects information about how its devices and channels are used under its own privacy policy. The developer of Family Calendar does not receive that information.

Children

The channel is meant for households and is not directed at children under 13. It does not knowingly collect personal information from anyone.
